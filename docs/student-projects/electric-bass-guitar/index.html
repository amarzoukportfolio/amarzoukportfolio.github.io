STUDENT PROJECTS / CNC · 2D & 3D design · Woodworking · Grade 8

Electric bass guitar.

A middle school student’s dream, developed through the design cycle into a fully functional electric bass over a school year.

For
Grade 8 · first woodworking project
Size
Full-size bass · standard 34 in (864 mm) scale
Materials
Mahogany body · wood stain and polyurethane · bass electronics kit
Machines
Laser cutter, CNC router, router table, spray gun, hand-sanding tools, soldering iron
Software
Adobe Illustrator · Shapr3D · VCarve (CAM)
Time
One school year
AInquiring and analyzingBDeveloping ideasCCreating the solutionDEvaluating

Process organized using the MYP Design cycle.

A / MYP DESIGN CYCLE

Inquiring and analyzing

A dream becomes a design brief

A middle school student came to me with a dream: to build an electric bass guitar. We turned that ambition into a design brief and worked through the design cycle over a school year.

Research before making

The student researched different bass body profiles and models, and chose one that suited his needs. That profile became the starting point for his design.

B / MYP DESIGN CYCLE

Developing ideas

Ideas in 2D and 3D

He developed the chosen profile as a 2D design in Adobe Illustrator. We then used Shapr3D for 3D modeling and VCarve for CAM, taking the design from digital models to fabrication.

C / MYP DESIGN CYCLE

Creating the solution

Prototyping

We first machined a prototype in plywood, which is a cheaper material. The footage shows the CNC router cutting the bass body outline, connecting the digital design to a physical trial piece. This piece was used as a prototype and later was used as a template for the table router to create rounded edges on the final product.

Machining the plywood prototype before the mahogany body.

CNC manufacturing

We used VCarve for CAM and a CNC router to machine the mahogany body. The wood in this footage is the final body, following the plywood prototype stage.

Machining the mahogany body on the CNC router.

Applying the wood stain

We used a spray gun to apply wood stain to the bass guitar body. The machined recesses for the pickups, controls and neck joint are still visible before the hardware is fitted.

Applying wood stain to the machined bass guitar body using a spray gun

Finishing and wiring the electronics

At this stage, we applied the polyurethane finish to the bass guitar body and prepared it for electronics installation. The body is held on a temporary support while the component recesses remain accessible.

Once the body was fully finished, we installed a bass electronics kit we had bought and soldered its wiring together.

Applying the polyurethane finish and preparing the body for electronics installation.

D / MYP DESIGN CYCLE

Evaluating

Testing the finished instrument

We tested the product as part of the design cycle and completed a fully functional electric bass. The photo shows the student testing it live with a band in the music room.

The student testing the bass live with a band in the music room. Play the GIF to see the instrument in use.

THE FINISHED WORK / RESULT

The result.

The completed electric bass, with its wooden body, strings, pickups, bridge and controls fitted.

The student testing the finished bass live with a band in the music room.

MY ROLE

How I supported the build.

This was his first woodworking project, so I broke the design cycle into small, manageable stages. For each stage, I worked with him on the relevant skill separately and trained him before he used it on the actual piece. That approach connected 2D and 3D design with the machine, hand-tool and finishing skills needed to build safely.

WHAT THE STUDENT LEARNED

Learning through making.

The student took an idea through the design cycle and finished with a working electric bass, connecting digital design with fabrication, finishing and testing. This project inspired him to keep designing and making. After moving to another school, he went on to build more electric guitars.
