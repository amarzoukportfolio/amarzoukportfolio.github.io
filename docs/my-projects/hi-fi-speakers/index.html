MY PROJECTS / Acoustic design · Product design · Vorm for MAWJ

Hi-Fi speakers & diffuser wall.

An eight-week design proposal for MAWJ, created through my studio Vorm. Custom speakers and a diffuser wall developed around a scanned space, its acoustics and the listener’s personal style.

A / MYP DESIGN CYCLE

Inquiring and analyzing

Start with the actual room

This project began with the space itself. I took dimensions on site and used Polycam to capture a 3D scan, then developed a room model in Shapr3D to study the setting for the speakers and diffuser wall.

The measured geometry gave the design a specific spatial context: the proposal was developed around the room in which the system would be used.

Interior visualization of the room model, showing the speakers and diffuser arrangement together.

Understand the listener

Alongside the room study, I explored the client’s preferred style and the character they wanted the space to express. This helped establish a visual direction for the cabinets, wall treatment and material palette.

The brief brought two ambitions together: a system designed in response to the room’s acoustics, and a personal environment that reflected its owner.

B / MYP DESIGN CYCLE

Developing ideas

Develop the room as a complete system

I used web-based acoustic calculation and simulation tools to inform the design in relation to the room. The speakers and diffuser wall were developed together, with the aim of making the most of the system’s components within the space and the user’s listening preferences.

Shapr3D allowed me to study the cabinet forms, wall arrangement and their relationship to the listening area as one coordinated proposal.

The speaker pair and central wall treatment, viewed from inside the proposed listening room.

Give the speaker a distinct identity

The cabinet was designed specifically for this project. Its proportions, horn form, grille and stand were developed as parts of one object, giving the speaker a strong identity within the room.

The exposed horn and contrasting surfaces make the speaker’s different elements visually legible, while the timber cabinet connects it to the wider wall composition.

Close-up visualization of the horn geometry and its transition into the speaker cabinet.

C / MYP DESIGN CYCLE

Creating the solution

Resolve the materials and detailing

The proposed material palette combines engineered wood, walnut and painted engineered wood. The natural timber and painted surfaces create contrast across the speakers and wall treatment while keeping the room visually connected.

I developed the CAD model in Shapr3D, working through the cabinetry, grille rhythm, support details and their relationship to the overall composition.

Cabinet visualization showing the wood grain, grille, metal support and small branding detail.

Bring the diffuser wall into the composition

The overall diffuser arrangement measures 4.8 × 3.9 m. Its central panel and flanking elements were designed as part of the room-specific acoustic proposal, while also forming a visual backdrop to the speakers.

Changes in depth and the alternating natural and painted surfaces give the wall a strong vertical rhythm. The two side details below show how that treatment carries across the composition.

Left wall diffuser: a close-up of the alternating surfaces and depth changes.

Right wall diffuser: the corresponding detail on the opposite side.

Carry MAWJ’s identity into the details

The centre panel includes the MAWJ logo as a small integrated detail. It connects the installation to the hi-fi company without competing with the larger rhythm of the wall.

Developed through my design studio, Vorm, the proposal brings the client’s personal style and MAWJ’s identity into the same design language.

4K close-up of the MAWJ logo integrated into the centre diffuser panel.

D / MYP DESIGN CYCLE

Evaluating

Review the complete proposal

The final room model brings the acoustic brief, speaker design, diffuser arrangement and material palette into one reviewable proposal. Interior views show the system from within the space, while close-ups make the cabinet and wall details easier to assess.

Completed over eight weeks, the project demonstrates my approach to designing across product and interior scales: begin with the real space and its user, then develop the individual elements as a connected whole.
