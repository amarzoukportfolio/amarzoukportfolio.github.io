A / MYP DESIGN CYCLE

Inquiring and analyzing

Design for the tools you have

I wanted a low pine bed with a floating appearance, designed and built at home. The practical constraint shaped the project from the start: the parts had to be manageable with a circular saw and a small set of hand tools.

The design needed a clear support structure, straightforward cuts and an assembly sequence that made sense outside a fully equipped workshop.

B / MYP DESIGN CYCLE

Developing ideas

Version 1 — establish the idea

The first Shapr3D model established the low platform, recessed support and tall headboard. Diagonal members formed the internal bracing, while a second view explored how the slats would sit over the structure.

This gave me the overall direction. The next challenge was to turn it into something practical to mark out, cut and assemble at home.

Version 1: the initial concept with diagonal internal bracing.

Version 1: the slatted platform shown over the initial structure.

Early design visualization: an upholstered treatment explored around the platform and headboard.

Version 2 — simplify the structure

I replaced the diagonal arrangement with a rectangular grid of longitudinal and transverse members. The revised geometry made the parts easier to define and reduced the number of angled intersections to resolve.

Keeping the support structure orthogonal brought the model closer to the way I could actually build it: straight components, repeatable cuts and accessible connections.

Version 2: a rectangular support grid replaces the diagonal arrangement.

Version 2: checking the relationship between the support grid and slats.

Version 3 — resolve the build

The third version brought the support grid, outer frame and slats into one resolved design. The inset base creates the floating effect, while the perimeter boards give the platform a clean outline.

I developed the construction around pine boards and an assembly that could be built in layers. The headboard and mattress were also modelled to check the complete composition before making the frame.

Version 3: the slatted platform and finished frame geometry.

The dimensioned drawing translates the model into a reference for cutting and assembly. It shows the support layout, slatted platform and headboard frame, with dimensions recorded in centimetres.

Planning these relationships before cutting helped connect each individual board to its position in the complete assembly.

C / MYP DESIGN CYCLE

Creating the solution

Prepare the pine components

Pine was the sole timber used for the bed. With the design resolved in Shapr3D, the work moved to preparing the boards for the frame, supports and platform.

The circular saw was the main cutting machine. The simplified design kept the making process centred on straight cuts and assembly with the limited tools available at home.

Pine boards laid out at home before assembly.

Assemble the support grid

The build photographs show the frame coming together in stages. The early assembly establishes the outer rectangle and central members; further cross-members then divide it into the rectangular bays developed in the model.

The separate headboard frame can also be seen alongside the base. Measuring and marking tools, a square and a cordless driver support the assembly work.

Early assembly: the first frame members, with the headboard frame behind.

Further assembly: additional members establish the support grid.

Build up the frame and perimeter

The next photographs show the deeper grid assembled from stacked boards, followed by the boards around the top perimeter. This sequence makes the relationship between the supporting base and the visible platform easy to see.

The recessed support carries the structure inward from the outer edges, creating the shadow gap that gives the bed its floating appearance.

The assembled pine support grid before the top perimeter is complete.

The top perimeter boards added around the support grid.

Complete the slatted platform

The slats complete the top surface, spanning the internal supports within the outer perimeter. The assembled platform was then photographed in the bedroom.

The build took two days. The result photograph below records the actual pine platform, with its structure and slat arrangement still visible.

D / MYP DESIGN CYCLE

Evaluating

Evaluate the design through the build

The strongest measure of this project was whether the digital design could be made with the tools available at home. Across three versions, the structure moved from diagonal bracing to an orthogonal grid, and then to a resolved assembly of pine boards.

The scaled prototype provided an earlier opportunity to test assembly and assess durability at model scale. The build photographs then show the design carried into the full-size pine platform. The project demonstrates a principle I bring to design teaching: develop the form, material choices and manufacturing process together, so the final design responds to the conditions in which it will be made.
