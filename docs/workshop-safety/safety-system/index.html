A / MYP DESIGN CYCLE

Inquiring and analyzing

Problem & design brief

I built a workshop safety system that brings machine danger levels, PPE, user permissions and maintenance schedules together. It gives me a consistent place to check who may use a machine, what training is required and where to find its instructions.

Research & ideation

I wrote the student instructions at a grade 7 reading level, using OSHA, UK HSE and BS 4163 alongside other safety references. The sheet has a separate Sources & Verification tab, so the reasoning behind an instruction is easier to trace.

C / MYP DESIGN CYCLE

Creating the solution

Building the system

I organized the Training & Safety Tracker by machine, category, danger level, student use, location, training requirement and PPE. Each machine row links to its instructions. For example, the table saw row is marked High, with student use set to No.

Machine rows showing danger level, student use, training requirements, PPE and instruction links

Student instructions

The instructions separate who may use the machine from what to do before, during and after work, plus emergency steps. The table-saw example makes staff-only operation explicit. I use machine inductions and competency sign-offs alongside these written instructions.

Actual table-saw instruction excerpt from the Safety Instructions tab

Maintenance that is easy to follow

Separate Maintenance – HS and Maintenance – MS tabs track frequency, last check, next due date, status, procedure and notes. Keeping the procedure beside the schedule helps me see both when a check is due and what it involves. Status updates automatically from the last check date, so anything due soon or overdue stands out.

Actual high-school maintenance tracker excerpt, with last check, next due date, status and procedures

D / MYP DESIGN CYCLE

Evaluating

Testing & development

Keeping permissions, instructions and maintenance in one connected sheet makes them easy to review together. High-risk machines such as the table saw are clearly marked staff-only, every machine links to its own instructions, and inductions and competency sign-offs back up the written guidance.
