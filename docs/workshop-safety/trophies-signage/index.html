A / MYP DESIGN CYCLE

Inquiring and analyzing

A recurring brief from the school

Throughout my years at Cairo American College, I have designed and built trophies, signs and graphics for the school. These requests return each year, giving me the opportunity to develop the work further with every new commission.

The briefs range from celebrating achievement to communicating information around campus. Each piece needs to belong to its event or setting while remaining connected to the school’s identity.

Dimensional lettering and colourful graphics installed on the school’s DHub wall.

B / MYP DESIGN CYCLE

Developing ideas

Give each event its own identity

An award should communicate what it celebrates. I develop the silhouette, lettering and graphic details around the occasion, using Adobe Illustrator to bring the elements into a clear visual composition.

The Spelling Bee trophies show that development over time. The 2024 design uses a star motif and the school identity; the 2026 version introduces a bee holding a book, an alphabet pattern and a honeycomb-shaped base. The event remains the same, but the design moves forward.

Spelling Bee 2024: a star-based design with clear acrylic and contrasting graphics.

Spelling Bee 2026: the bee, book, alphabet pattern and honeycomb base form an event-specific design.

Design a coordinated award family

Many commissions involve a set of awards. I develop the pieces together so they share a visual language while the wording and proportions distinguish each category or placing.

The talent-show trophies use clear acrylic, a rising star and engraved linework. The 2025 set below and the 2026 set at the top of the page show how a recognizable design can be revisited and refined for another year.

The 2025 Middle School Talent Show trophy set, with coordinated Best Performance, second-place and third-place awards.

C / MYP DESIGN CYCLE

Creating the solution

From Illustrator to layered acrylic

Adobe Illustrator is my main design tool for this work. I prepare the shapes, lettering and graphics for the laser cutter and vinyl cutter, then bring the fabricated elements together in the finished piece.

Acrylic sheets are the main material. Clear areas, coloured layers and contrasting graphics allow the same workshop processes to serve both detailed awards and larger signs. The crossing sign below shows how separate graphic elements become one clear composition.

The school crossing sign on the workbench, showing its yellow background, black graphic layers and school name.

Take the graphics into the school environment

For signage, the message needs to remain clear in its intended setting. The photographs show the no-horn sign on the workbench and later installed outside the school, with a bold symbol and contrasting text.

This work connects graphic design with physical fabrication: the artwork becomes an object that has to read beyond the design screen.

No-horn sign on the workbench, ready for installation.

The completed no-horn sign installed outside Cairo American College.

Make recognition specific to its purpose

The collection also includes core-values awards, staff appreciation pieces, sporting recognition and commemorative work. These commissions call for different symbols, messages and compositions rather than one repeated trophy shape.

The core-values award combines the school’s visual identity with a clear acrylic form. The appreciation piece takes a different approach, giving the message itself the strongest presence.

Honor of the Eagle / Core Values award, with the school’s symbols integrated into the design.

An appreciation award whose composition is centred on its message.

D / MYP DESIGN CYCLE

Evaluating

Return each year with a better design

The school asks me to undertake this work each year, and I use each new request to develop and upgrade the designs. The recurring commissions create an ongoing cycle of design, fabrication and refinement.

Taken together, the trophies and installed signs show a sustained contribution to school life: recognizing people, marking occasions and making the school’s identity visible in its everyday surroundings.

The completed Cairo American College crossing sign installed outside the school.
